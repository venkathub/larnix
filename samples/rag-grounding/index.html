01 / THE POLICY PUZZLE

A confident answer.
An expired policy.

BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Know that retrieval finds candidate passages and a language model produces text from its input. The full curriculum prerequisites are M12-L04 (context engineering) and M16-L04 (retrieval). This sample recaps the concepts it needs; no API key or coding is required to explore.

Outcome: trace an answer to its sources and locate where missing evidence disappeared. Allow 2–4 additional hours for the optional production exercise.

Return to our fictional camera-rental shop. Until September, customers had seven days to return a standard rental. On October 1, the shop changed that window to three days. The late fee stayed at ₹200 per extra calendar day.

A customer asks, “If I pick up a camera today, what is my return window and daily late fee?” An assistant that answers from old information might say seven days. The sentence could be fluent, specific and wrong for this customer.

The problem is not how to make the sentence sound better. The assistant needs the applicable policy in front of it. Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, combines finding external information with producing an answer using that information.

Think of a colleague with a policy folder

Imagine a new help-desk colleague. You would not ask them to memorize every policy and hope nothing changes. You would give them access to the policy folder, ask them to find the relevant pages, and expect them to say which page supports their answer.

There are still ways to go wrong. They might open an old page, overlook a fee schedule, or read two notices that disagree. Access to the folder helps; careful evidence handling still matters. Our assistant faces corresponding problems at retrieval, context selection and answer generation.

The analogy has a limit: a language model is not a colleague who reliably recognizes uncertainty. It can make unsupported claims even when useful evidence is supplied. We therefore need explicit checks and an evaluated policy for abstention—declining to give a definitive answer when the available evidence is insufficient.

MAKE A PREDICTIONThe assistant cites a seven-day policy that expired in September. What does that citation establish?

DETERMINISTIC TEACHING LAB

Start with “Current policy”. Follow R1, R2 and F1 through the pipeline. Then try “Tight context”: the fee is retrieved but no longer reaches the answer. “Retrieval miss” creates the same missing fact at an earlier stage.

This lab uses fictional policy text, fixed candidate order and a template-based mock generator. It does not call a language model or measure retrieval quality. The reference date is fixed at October 12, 2026. Context capacity is counted in whole passages, not model tokens.

      03 / INSIDE THE PIPELINE

      Six responsibilities.
      One answer.

      Use the opening question and default controls for this walkthrough. It asks for two facts: a return window and a daily late fee. A complete answer needs evidence for both. A document that mentions rentals but supplies neither fact will not fill that gap.

      1

      Retrieve candidates

      The retriever returns passages that might help. In our deliberately awkward fixture, the first three are R1 (old return policy), R2 (new return policy), and F1 (fee schedule). Top K means the maximum number of candidates returned; it does not mean every result is useful.

      A real retriever may use keyword search, vector similarity, or both. Our fixed ordering keeps the experiment reproducible. It is not a learned relevance ranking.

      2

      Select applicable evidence

      R1 expired on September 30. The question concerns a rental picked up on October 12, so the validity filter removes R1. R2 became effective on October 1, and F1 is still effective. Both remain eligible.

      “Newest” is not a universal rule. An older contract may apply to an earlier transaction. This example uses a fixed current-date question and simple inclusive validity intervals. Real selection also needs scope, authority and access controls.

      3

      Construct the context

      Context is the material supplied to the generator for this request: instructions, the question and selected evidence. With two available passage slots, we pack R2 and F1, keeping their identifiers attached.

      A real system must budget tokens for instructions, history, evidence and the answer. This lab uses passage slots so you can see the boundary without a tokenizer. More retrieved passages do not automatically mean more evidence fits.

      4

      Generate from the supplied evidence

      Our mock reads the known policy fields and assembles an answer: “The return window is 3 calendar days from pickup. The late fee is ₹200 per calendar day beyond that window.” A real language model would generate wording from its context and could introduce errors our deterministic template cannot.

      5

      Attach and check citations

      The return-window claim points to R2; the fee claim points to F1. Click a citation in the lab to inspect the actual passage. A reference to a real document is only the beginning: check that its text supports the particular claim and applies to this question.

      6

      Abstain when the answer cannot be justified

      If one required fact is absent, this sample withholds the complete answer and names the missing evidence. If visible policies disagree about a required fact, it asks for resolution. Those are application policies we chose for the exercise.

      A different product might give a clearly labeled partial answer. Either way, missing evidence should remain visible. Asking a model to “be confident” does not supply the missing policy.

      THE WORKED TRACE

      Retrieve R1, R2, F1 → remove expired R1 → pack R2 + F1 → answer “3 days, ₹200/day” → cite each claim. Change any boundary and inspect what survives.

      04 / WHEN EVIDENCE FAILS

      Same missing answer.
      Different repairs.

      Try the scenarios before reading their explanations. The important observation is not just whether the final answer changes. Look for the earliest stage where the information needed for a correct answer becomes unavailable.

      The fee exists, but retrieval misses it

      In “Retrieval miss,” top K is 2. R1 and R2 arrive; F1 never reaches the candidate list. After removing the expired R1, the generator has only a return window. For this fixture, raising K to 3 recovers the fee. In a real system, also inspect the query, index contents and ranking before treating a larger K as the fix.

      The fee is retrieved, but context drops it

      In “Tight context,” all three candidates arrive, but only R2 fits after filtering. F1 is explicitly marked as omitted. More retrieval will not fix the one-slot constraint. You might change the evidence budget, compress passages without losing meaning, or select evidence to cover each part of the question.

      The wrong answer has a perfectly traceable citation

      In “Expired but cited,” R2 is unavailable and the validity filter is off. R1 and F1 produce a complete, cited answer of seven days and ₹200 per day. The lab’s separate date warning exposes why this is wrong for today. Re-enable the filter: the answer now abstains because it has no applicable return window.

      This is the central distinction: support in a source and applicability to the question are separate checks. A citation can show exactly where an outdated answer came from.

      Two current policies disagree

      In “Conflicting policies,” R2 says three days and R3 says five. Both have the same effective start date and equal assumed authority. The mock refuses to pick one. A real policy owner must resolve this conflict or supply an explicit precedence rule. Do not silently let retrieval order become company policy.

      Reduce context slots to 2 in this scenario. R3 falls out of context, so the mock answers from R2 and F1 again. That is not proof the conflict vanished; it shows the limit of checking only visible evidence.

      The folder does not contain the answer

      “Unsupported question” asks about a damage deposit. None of the passages contains that fact. Increasing K or context capacity cannot recover information that is absent from the source collection. Find an authoritative deposit policy or send the question to someone who can answer it.

      All available passages are in context, but none specifies the deposit. What should the assistant do?

      A plausible number is not a supported number.

      Does RAG eliminate hallucination? +

      No. Retrieval can miss evidence, context can contain untrusted or inapplicable text, and a generator can misread or embellish what it receives. This lesson isolates evidence flow; it does not establish that a real model follows the mock’s rules. Evaluate supported claims, correct abstention and useful task completion with representative questions.

      Is RAG the same as fine-tuning? +

      No. This pipeline brings external passages into a request at answer time. Fine-tuning changes model parameters through training. They can be combined, but updating a policy in this source folder is not training the generator. A real system must still refresh any index and caches that contain the old policy.

      05 / BUILD AND VERIFY

      Build a policy assistant
      you can inspect.

      CPU ONLY · MOCK FIRST

      Your assignment

      Implement this small pipeline with the downloadable, versioned fixtures. Start with a mock generator so that evidence handling can be checked without model variability. A local or hosted model is an optional second experiment.

      1. Load the policy passages, including their IDs, versions and validity intervals.
      2. Log candidates, exclusions, context IDs, answer status and claim-to-source mappings for each case.
      3. Compare your results with the fixed expected outcomes. Include missing evidence, expired sources, contradictions and an unsupported question.
      4. Write a short decision note: your abstention policy, where date filtering belongs, what the mock cannot demonstrate, and what changes when a real model is used.
      Python starter ↓Policies + expected cases ↓

      Keep both downloads in one directory. The Python starter contains TODOs; the fixtures include hand-checkable expected results.

      DEFINITION OF DONE

      • Every emitted citation resolves to a passage actually supplied in context.
      • Required facts are checked individually; irrelevant passages cannot fill a missing fact.
      • Conflicting visible values trigger an explicit resolution policy.
      • The expired-but-cited failure is reproduced and caught by a separate date check.
      • A fixture report records expected and actual results, including abstentions.

      Do not count abstaining on every question as success. Supported questions must receive the correct answer too.

      BEFORE A REAL DEPLOYMENT

      Add source permissions, ingestion and deletion handling, real token budgets, prompt-injection defenses, and evaluation of model-generated claims. Documents are evidence, not instructions that grant new authority. Record source, index, prompt and model versions so failures can be reproduced.

      06 / EXPLAIN IT BACK

      Which component
      would you change?

      INTERVIEW PROMPT

      “Which part of a RAG pipeline would you change for missing evidence?”

      Answer using the two fee failures. The final symptom is identical, but one trace calls for retrieval work and the other calls for context work.

      Read a strong answer and rubric +

      “I would first find the earliest stage where the required evidence is absent. If the source is missing or outdated, I would investigate ingestion and freshness. If it is indexed but absent from candidates, I would inspect retrieval and ranking. If retrieved but omitted from the prompt, I would inspect context selection and budgeting. If valid evidence is present but the answer ignores or contradicts it, I would investigate generation and claim verification. I would keep explicit abstention and test both answerable and unanswerable cases.”

      • 0 / 3: Proposes a change without identifying the evidence gap.
      • 1 / 3: Recognizes that retrieval can fail.
      • 2 / 3: Separates source, retrieval, context and generation failures.
      • 3 / 3: Uses a trace to diagnose the boundary, proposes a targeted repair and verifies it with positive and negative cases.

      07 / KEEP THE KEY IDEAS

      Follow the evidence.
      Then trust the answer.

      1. RAG brings external information into answering. The generator can only use supplied evidence reliably if each preceding stage does its job.
      2. Retrieval and context are different boundaries. A passage can be found and still never reach the generator.
      3. A citation needs scrutiny. Its source must support the claim and apply to the question’s date and scope.
      4. Missing or conflicting evidence needs an explicit policy. Abstention is useful when warranted, but indiscriminate abstention is also a failure.
      5. Diagnose with a trace. Find where evidence disappeared before choosing a repair.

      A small glossary

      Chunk / passage
      A piece of a source document stored or selected as a retrieval unit.
      Retriever
      The component that finds candidate evidence for a question.
      Top K
      The maximum number of candidates requested from retrieval.
      Context
      The input supplied to the generator, including the question, instructions and selected evidence.
      Grounding
      Connecting answer claims to applicable evidence rather than relying on unsupported assertions.
      Abstention
      Withholding a definitive answer when the application’s evidence requirements are not met.
