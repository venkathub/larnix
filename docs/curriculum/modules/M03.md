**Delivery:** preview · full lesson contract: not_met · interaction: tested_specialist_engine · artifact: learner_starter

**Prerequisites:** [M02-L01](M02.md#m02-l01)

**Learning outcome:** Compute similarity and diagnose shape or normalization mistakes.

**Topics:** dot product; norms; cosine; matrix shapes; projections.

**Study estimate:** 1–2 hours guided study + 1–3 hours exercise. These are uncalibrated learner-effort estimates, not measured timings or an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [MATH: Dive into Deep Learning preliminaries](https://d2l.ai/chapter_preliminaries/index.html) — Linear algebra, calculus, automatic differentiation, probability.
- [FAISS: Faiss research and documentation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki) — Similarity metrics, approximate indexes, IVF, HNSW, quantization.

### Interview question

When do cosine and dot-product rankings disagree?

**A strong answer addresses:** Vector magnitude; normalization; model objective.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Create and execute a hand-checkable similarity notebook for five synthetic document vectors.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Include zero vectors and unequal norms.
- Compare manual and library calculations.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.
- Baseline or hand-checkable fixture.
- Results or expected-results table including a failure case.
- Decision and limitations note.

**Resources:** CPU/local fixtures or a mocked provider; live service use is optional.

## M03-L05 — Design causal questions and controlled experiments

**Delivery:** planned · full lesson contract: not_met · interaction: none · artifact: none

**Prerequisites:** [M03-L04](M03.md#m03-l04)

**Learning outcome:** Separate a predictive association from an intervention effect and pre-specify a randomized test.

**Topics:** causal inference; confounding and DAGs; randomization unit; A/B test design; power and minimum detectable effect; sample-ratio mismatch; sequential peeking.

**Study estimate:** 2–3 hours guided study + 3–5 hours exercise. These are uncalibrated learner-effort estimates, not measured timings or an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [CAUSAL: DoWhy documentation](https://www.pywhy.org/dowhy/main/index.html) — Identification, estimation, causal assumptions and refutation.
- [ABTEST: Online Evaluation for Information Retrieval](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/ftir-online-evaluation-final-journal.pdf) — Controlled experiments, metrics, historical policy evaluation.

### Interview question

Why does a model predicting repeat customers not identify who benefits from a discount?

**A strong answer addresses:** Confounding versus intervention; Choose an estimand and randomization unit; Predefine stopping and guardrails.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Simulate a rental reminder experiment with repeat customers and an unequal assignment bug; write an analysis plan before calculating outcomes.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- State the causal assumptions and estimand.
- Detect the assignment imbalance.
- Report uncertainty and guardrail results without repeated uncorrected peeking.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned code or analysis with environment and input checksum.
- Hand-checkable baseline and comparison outputs.
- Injected failure, diagnosis and corrected rerun.
- Decision, resource assumptions and limitations.

**Resources:** Small original CPU/local fixture; hosted services and accelerators optional. Record simulation, replay or measurement honestly.

## M03-L06 — Compare bandits and uplift decisions

**Delivery:** planned · full lesson contract: not_met · interaction: none · artifact: none

**Prerequisites:** [M03-L05](M03.md#m03-l05)

**Learning outcome:** Explain why optimizing observed rewards requires exploration and why treatment effect differs from response likelihood.

**Topics:** contextual bandits; exploration versus exploitation; logged propensities; inverse propensity weighting; overlap; uplift and heterogeneous treatment effects.

**Study estimate:** 2–4 hours guided study + 4–6 hours exercise. These are uncalibrated learner-effort estimates, not measured timings or an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [BANDIT: Vowpal Wabbit contextual bandits](https://vowpalwabbit.org/docs/vowpal_wabbit/python/latest/tutorials/python_Contextual_bandits_and_Vowpal_Wabbit.html) — Logged action probabilities, exploration and partial feedback.
- [UPLIFT: EconML documentation](https://www.pywhy.org/EconML/) — Heterogeneous treatment effects and policy decisions.
- [ABTEST: Online Evaluation for Information Retrieval](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/ftir-online-evaluation-final-journal.pdf) — Controlled experiments, metrics, historical policy evaluation.

### Interview question

When does an offline policy estimate become unreliable despite a large log?

**A strong answer addresses:** Missing action support; Large inverse-propensity weights; Unobserved outcomes are not negative labels.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Construct a small synthetic offer log with known action probabilities; compare an always-popular policy with an importance-weighted estimate and an uplift decision table.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Verify the estimator by hand on a small fixture.
- Reject zero-support actions.
- Show a high-variance case and distinguish simulated from measured benefit.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned code or analysis with environment and input checksum.
- Hand-checkable baseline and comparison outputs.
- Injected failure, diagnosis and corrected rerun.
- Decision, resource assumptions and limitations.

**Resources:** Small original CPU/local fixture; hosted services and accelerators optional. Record simulation, replay or measurement honestly.
