## M31-L01 — Classify risks and responsibilities

**Delivery:** planned · full lesson contract: not_met · interaction: none · artifact: none

**Prerequisites:** [M01-L04](M01.md#m01-l04), [M06-L04](M06.md#m06-l04), [M25-L04](M25.md#m25-l04), [M30-L01](M30.md#m30-l01)

**Learning outcome:** Assign owners and review depth according to likely impact.

**Topics:** risk assessment; intended use; affected parties; accountability; human oversight.

**Study estimate:** 1–2 hours guided study + 2–3 hours exercise. These are learner-effort estimates, not an implementation schedule.

**Estimate basis:** Study classify risks and responsibilities; build or analyze: Draft a risk register for rental eligibility assistance using fictional applicants. Verify: Separate decision support from automated decisions. Stated prerequisites met; small local fixture or explicitly labeled replay; excludes large training runs and cloud provisioning. Confidence: low; editorial estimate, not learner-measured.

### Primary readings

- [NIST: NIST AI risk management framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — Governance, risk identification, measurement and management.

### Interview question

**A strong answer addresses:** Affected groups; error severity; downstream use.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

**Acceptance criteria:**

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.

## M31-L02 — Evaluate fairness and accessibility

**Delivery:** planned · full lesson contract: not_met · interaction: none · artifact: none

**Prerequisites:** [M31-L01](M31.md#m31-l01)

**Learning outcome:** Choose fairness measures in context and explain their tradeoffs.

**Topics:** group slices; fairness metrics; representativeness; accessibility; contestability; explainability and contestability; SHAP and LIME limitations; feature attribution is not causality.

**Study estimate:** 2–3 hours guided study + 3–5 hours exercise. These are learner-effort estimates, not an implementation schedule.

**Estimate basis:** Study evaluate fairness and accessibility; build or analyze: Run a synthetic eligibility fairness audit and design an accessible appeal flow. Verify: Report subgroup uncertainty. Stated prerequisites met; small local fixture or explicitly labeled replay; excludes large training runs and cloud provisioning. Confidence: low; editorial estimate, not learner-measured.

### Primary readings

- [NIST: NIST AI risk management framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — Governance, risk identification, measurement and management.
- [HAX: Microsoft human AI interaction guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/) — User expectations, corrections, feedback, recovery.
- [SHAP: A Unified Approach to Interpreting Model Predictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07874) — Feature attribution and additive explanations.
- [LIME: Why Should I Trust You? Explaining the Predictions of Any Classifier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04938) — Local surrogate explanations and their limitations.

### Interview question

Why can two reasonable fairness metrics conflict?

**A strong answer addresses:** Base rates; thresholds; competing objectives.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Run a synthetic eligibility fairness audit and design an accessible appeal flow.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Report subgroup uncertainty.
- Explain metric choice and include a usable correction path.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.
- Baseline or hand-checkable fixture.
- Results or expected-results table including a failure case.
- Decision and limitations note.

**Resources:** CPU/local fixtures or a mocked provider; live service use is optional.
