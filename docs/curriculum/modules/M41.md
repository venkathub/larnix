Four lesson specifications follow. Each lesson will use a concrete problem, prediction, interactive explanation, worked example, inline checks and failure investigation before production and interview practice. Follow the teaching design for accessibility and original visual treatment. These interactions are planned, not implemented. Complete the evidence package for each exercise; live deployment is not required. Later lessons may introduce an additional cross-module prerequisite.

## Module outcomes

- Convert an ambiguous prompt into quantified assumptions and an initial design.
- Defend one architecture against at least one credible alternative.
- Find the weakest assumption through adversarial design review.
- Present evidence sufficient for a controlled pilot.

## M41-L01 — Turn requirements into architecture

**Prerequisites:** [M27-L04](M27.md#m27-l04), [M29-L04](M29.md#m29-l04), [M30-L04](M30.md#m30-l04), [M31-L04](M31.md#m31-l04), [M32-L04](M32.md#m32-l04), [M40-L04](M40.md#m40-l04)

**Learning outcome:** Convert an ambiguous prompt into quantified assumptions and an initial design.

**Topics:** functional requirements; quality targets; constraints; traffic; capacity; boundaries.

**Study estimate:** 1–2 hours guided study + 2–4 hours exercise. These are learner-effort estimates, not an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [FRAME: Google ML problem framing](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/problem-framing) — Problem suitability, objectives, success metrics.
- [SRE: Google SRE book](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/) — Service objectives, overload, monitoring, incident response, reliability testing.

### Interview question

What would you clarify before designing a company-wide knowledge assistant?

**A strong answer addresses:** Users; data rights; freshness; load; quality; budget.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Write a system-design brief for a fictional multi-tenant equipment platform.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- State assumptions and non-goals.
- Calculate rough storage, request and token volumes.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.
- Baseline or hand-checkable fixture.
- Results or expected-results table including a failure case.
- Decision and limitations note.

**Resources:** CPU/local fixtures or a mocked provider; live service use is optional.

## M41-L03 — Stress the design

**Prerequisites:** [M41-L02](M41.md#m41-l02)

**Learning outcome:** Find the weakest assumption through adversarial design review.

**Topics:** burst traffic; dependency loss; tenant isolation; deletions; migration; cost spikes.

**Study estimate:** 1–2 hours guided study + 2–4 hours exercise. These are learner-effort estimates, not an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [SRE: Google SRE book](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/) — Service objectives, overload, monitoring, incident response, reliability testing.
- [NIST: NIST AI risk management framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — Governance, risk identification, measurement and management.

### Interview question

What fails first when traffic grows tenfold?

**A strong answer addresses:** Capacity; queues; quotas; storage; human review.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Run a tabletop design review with load, deletion and provider-outage scenarios.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Provide a detection signal and recovery step for each failure.
- Revise capacity and cost assumptions.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.
- Baseline or hand-checkable fixture.
- Results or expected-results table including a failure case.
- Decision and limitations note.

**Resources:** CPU/local fixtures or a mocked provider; live service use is optional.

## M41-L04 — Assemble a production readiness case

**Prerequisites:** [M41-L03](M41.md#m41-l03)

**Learning outcome:** Present evidence sufficient for a controlled pilot.

**Topics:** quality gates; runbooks; ownership; rollout; rollback; risk register; cost model.

**Study estimate:** 1–2 hours guided study + 2–4 hours exercise. These are learner-effort estimates, not an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [FSDL: Full Stack Deep Learning curriculum](https://fullstackdeeplearning.com/course/2022/) — Experiment management, testing, data, deployment, monitoring, project design.
- [SRE: Google SRE book](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/) — Service objectives, overload, monitoring, incident response, reliability testing.
- [NIST: NIST AI risk management framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — Governance, risk identification, measurement and management.

### Interview question

What would make you delay launch despite a successful demo?

**A strong answer addresses:** Unmeasured failures; missing ownership; rollback gaps.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Create a capstone design dossier for the approved architecture.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Include evaluation, security, operations and economics evidence.
- Label untested assumptions and define go/no-go criteria.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.
- Baseline or hand-checkable fixture.
- Results or expected-results table including a failure case.
- Decision and limitations note.

**Resources:** CPU/local fixtures or a mocked provider; live service use is optional.

## Module completion gate

Submit all four lesson artifacts, answer the four interview questions, and explain one failure-and-recovery case. A reviewer checks acceptance criteria and labels results as designed, simulated, replayed or measured. A plan or replay is not proof of production performance.

Readings support concepts. Larnix's lesson sequencing, scenarios, questions and acceptance criteria are original curriculum design. Recheck relevant source sections and pin implementation versions when writing the actual lessons.
