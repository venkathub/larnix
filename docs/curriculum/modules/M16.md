## M16-L01 — Build lexical and dense baselines

**Delivery:** planned · full lesson contract: not_met · interaction: none · artifact: none

**Prerequisites:** [M13-L04](M13.md#m13-l04), [M15-L04](M15.md#m15-l04)

**Learning outcome:** Compare lexical and vector retrieval on distinct query types.

**Topics:** BM25; inverted indexes; semantic search; query analysis; recall@k.

**Study estimate:** 2–3 hours guided study + 3–5 hours exercise. These are uncalibrated learner-effort estimates, not measured timings or an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [SEARCH: Sentence Transformers retrieve and rerank](https://sbert.net/examples/sentence_transformer/applications/retrieve_rerank/README.html) — Bi-encoder candidates and cross-encoder ranking.
- [BM25: The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond](https://www.staff.city.ac.uk/~sbrp622/papers/foundations_bm25_review.pdf) — Term weighting, length normalization and probabilistic retrieval.

### Interview question

Why does BM25 remain useful beside dense retrieval?

**A strong answer addresses:** Exact terms; rare identifiers; transparency.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Implement keyword and dense retrieval baselines for original policy questions and equipment codes.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Use relevance judgments.
- Report recall@k by query type and absent-answer cases.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.
- Baseline or hand-checkable fixture.
- Results or expected-results table including a failure case.
- Decision and limitations note.

**Resources:** CPU/local fixtures or a mocked provider; live service use is optional.

## M16-L04 — Evaluate retrieval as a service

**Delivery:** planned · full lesson contract: not_met · interaction: none · artifact: none

**Prerequisites:** [M16-L03](M16.md#m16-l03)

**Learning outcome:** Design retrieval evaluation independently of answer generation.

**Topics:** relevance labels; MRR; nDCG; freshness; query slices; latency budgets.

**Study estimate:** 2–3 hours guided study + 4–6 hours exercise. These are uncalibrated learner-effort estimates, not measured timings or an implementation schedule.

### Primary readings

- [SEARCH: Sentence Transformers retrieve and rerank](https://sbert.net/examples/sentence_transformer/applications/retrieve_rerank/README.html) — Bi-encoder candidates and cross-encoder ranking.
- [BM25: The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond](https://www.staff.city.ac.uk/~sbrp622/papers/foundations_bm25_review.pdf) — Term weighting, length normalization and probabilistic retrieval.
- [ABTEST: Online Evaluation for Information Retrieval](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/ftir-online-evaluation-final-journal.pdf) — Controlled experiments, metrics, historical policy evaluation.

### Interview question

Why can good average retrieval hide a production failure?

**A strong answer addresses:** Tail queries; stale sources; permissions; no-answer traffic.

Score 0–3 using the [shared interview rubric](../README.md#interview-rubric).

### Production exercise

Build a retrieval scorecard with head, tail, multilingual and permission-sensitive queries.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Define metric denominators and cutoffs.
- Set slice-specific regression gates.

**Evidence to submit:**

- Versioned artifact or design specification.
- Baseline or hand-checkable fixture.
- Results or expected-results table including a failure case.
- Decision and limitations note.

**Resources:** CPU/local fixtures or a mocked provider; live service use is optional.

## Module completion gate

Submit every lesson artifact, answer its interview question, and explain one failure-and-recovery case. A reviewer checks acceptance criteria and labels results as designed, simulated, replayed or measured. A plan or replay is not proof of production performance.
